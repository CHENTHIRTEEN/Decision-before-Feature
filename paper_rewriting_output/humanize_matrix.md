# Humanize Matrix

The configured tier is `medium`; D1--D4 are therefore treated as required dimensions and D5 as advisory. Changes below preserve technical definitions and evidence boundaries rather than targeting a promised detector outcome.

| Row ID | Manuscript Unit | AI Pattern Found | Detection Dim | Severity | Applied Change | Expected Effect | Teaching Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H01 | Whole manuscript, especially section openings | Repeated “This paper/This section” openings and equally sized declarative paragraphs created a predictable cadence | D1 sentence structure | high | Varied openings among problem statements, contrasts, equations, and direct RQ framing; retained short boundary sentences beside longer technical explanations | Increases sentence and paragraph rhythm variation without changing claims | Structural variation should come from argumentative function, not ornamental synonym replacement |
| H02 | Abstract | Five pending RQ statements naturally repeat the same grammatical frame | D2 paragraph similarity | high | Kept the required five TBD slots but varied what each sentence foregrounds: prevalence, model comparison, policy trade-off, suite evidence, or association | Reduces repetitive local n-gram patterns while preserving one-to-one TBD mapping | Evidence contracts may repeat labels, but the surrounding syntax can reflect the different scientific question |
| H03 | Introduction | Dense sequences of abstract nouns could make the prose read uniformly compressed | D3 information density | medium | Split resource accounting, trajectory evidence, generalization risk, and task distinction into separate paragraphs with concrete examples and citations | Balances technical density and reader orientation | One conceptual burden per paragraph is safer than deleting necessary technical terms |
| H04 | Introduction and Related Work transitions | Formulaic connectors such as “Furthermore/Moreover/Therefore” could dominate progression | D4 connector frequency | high | Used content-bearing pivots such as “That prerequisite is not innocuous,” “At the same time,” and direct contrast sentences; removed unnecessary enumerative connectors | Lowers connector density and makes transitions depend on scientific logic | A transition is strongest when it names the changing comparison axis |
| H05 | Related Work subsection endings | Every stream risked ending with the same “does not address our problem” template | D2 paragraph similarity | medium | Varied endings by the actual missing axis: decision time, cost target, information source, transition, or split | Reduces template repetition and sharpens synthesis | Repetition is unnecessary when each literature stream leaves a different gap |
| H06 | Related Work terminology | Adjacent concepts—trajectory ELA, optimizer internals, behavior, landscape query—could be used interchangeably | D5 term-context matching | medium | Assigned each term a fixed role and added the comparison table across decision target, evidence time, extra FEs, internal inputs, and transition | Improves local semantic consistency | Controlled terminology is a scientific safeguard, not merely a style choice |
| H07 | Problem Formulation | Long equation-explanation sentences carried sign, normalization, cost, and caveat at once | D1 sentence structure | high | Placed sign interpretation after the raw difference, cost weights after the utility equation, and score semantics after the deployment rule | Creates natural short/long sentence alternation and makes each equation consequence visible | Mathematical prose should explain one implication at a time |
| H08 | Method behavior subsection | Repeated “is computed/defined as” constructions made dense definitions mechanically parallel | D1 sentence structure | medium | Alternated equations with rationale, endpoint interpretation, and exclusion statements while preserving exact formulas | Breaks monotony without obscuring definitions | Vary exposition around formulas, not the formal definitions themselves |
| H09 | Method and Experimental Setup | Lists of protocol choices risked homogeneous information packing | D3 information density | high | Converted only genuinely comparative objects into tables (behavior groups, RQs, queries) and kept causal/order explanations in prose | Lets tables carry repeated fields while prose explains why choices matter | Tables are useful when columns express a stable mapping, not as decoration |
| H10 | Experimental Setup | Earlier local file and generation details generated highly specific but irrelevant density | D3 information density | high | Replaced storage/execution narrative with estimands, populations, budgets, contrasts, and inferential units | Concentrates detail on what a reviewer can evaluate scientifically | Reproducibility is not proportional to the number of local filenames disclosed |
| H11 | Experimental Setup subsection sequence | Repeated introductory formulas (“The following…”, “We use…”) made the section list-like | D4 connector frequency | medium | Opened subsections directly with the benchmark, query condition, opportunity definition, policy set, or inferential rule | Removes low-information transitions | A named scientific object can usually serve as its own transition |
| H12 | Results opening | Project-status narration created a fourth-wall voice and formulaic administrative phrasing | D5 term-context matching | high | Replaced withdrawal/regeneration/file language with a neutral statement that confirmatory estimates are unavailable and structures retain evidence-specific TBDs | Aligns vocabulary with journal evidence reporting | Reader-facing text should describe epistemic status, not the author's workflow status |
| H13 | Results RQ1--RQ5 | Identical table–figure–statistics–answer scaffolds can sound machine-generated even when scientifically useful | D2 paragraph similarity | high | Preserved the validation sequence but customized table columns, figure purpose, inferential conditions, and boundary sentence to each RQ | Retains auditability while reducing repetitive surface form | Repeated scientific logic need not imply repeated sentences or captions |
| H14 | Results captions | Generic captions could merely restate table titles | D3 information density | medium | Added direction, replication unit, selection role, coverage, or interpretation boundary specific to each display | Improves standalone informativeness | A caption should tell the reader how to read the evidence, not only what object is shown |
| H15 | Results answer paragraphs | Repeated “insufficient evidence” phrasing risked a flat cadence | D1 sentence structure | medium | Varied the leading clause while retaining precise missing comparison, interval, or coverage condition | Makes pending conclusions distinct without weakening the evidence boundary | Variation is acceptable only after the required evidence condition remains explicit |
| H16 | Discussion | Six TBD subsections could become a sequence of nearly identical disclaimers | D2 paragraph similarity | high | Assigned each paragraph a different future interpretive burden: scope, metric conflict, joint resource/performance evidence, suite coverage, non-causality, or validity sensitivity | Reduces structural sameness and prepares non-redundant final interpretation | Limitations are clearer when attached to the specific conclusion they constrain |
| H17 | Reproducibility | Procedural enumerations could become a command manual | D4 connector frequency | high | Kept one numbered list for scientific dependencies and used direct prose elsewhere; removed CLI-style sequencing | Lowers transition clutter and preserves necessary order once | Use enumeration only when order changes validity |
| H18 | Reproducibility terminology | “Check,” “output,” and engineering labels could dominate the scientific register | D5 term-context matching | medium | Used state restoration, permutation invariance, FE accounting, feature coverage, preprocessing scope, and reconstruction of statistical quantities | Aligns terms with actual scientific operations | Name the object and criterion being examined rather than relying on generic process nouns |
| H19 | Conclusion | Five TBD statements can sound templated and over-defensive | D1 sentence structure | medium | Varied sentence structure around the distinct RQ evidence while keeping one concise method statement and one final query-scope boundary | Produces a more natural close without inventing a synthesis | A conclusion can be incomplete yet precise when each missing answer has a named requirement |
| H20 | Whole manuscript claim vocabulary | Repeated universal-sounding words (“proves,” “generalizes,” “negligible”) would create both stylistic and scientific risk | D3 information density | high | Replaced them with scoped verbs—defines, evaluates, is associated with, remains unverified—and required effects/intervals for future empirical verbs | Removes generic promotional language and increases evidential specificity | Claim calibration is part of style because readers infer credibility from verb strength |
