\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:experimental-setup}

\subsection{Research questions and evaluation design}

The empirical study addresses the five prespecified questions in
Table~\ref{tab:research-questions}.  Each question is linked to a target
distribution, a comparison set, and uncertainty summaries that respect the
nested dependence of decision states within runs, problems, and benchmark
functions.  A benchmark function, rather than a state, run, transformed
instance, or function--dimension combination, is the top-level statistical
unit.

\subsection{Internal benchmark and grouped-by-function partition}

The internal benchmark is the noiseless BBOB suite.  The training set comprises
functions 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11, 12, 15, 16, 17, 18, 20, 21, 22, and 23;
functions 5, 9, 13, 14, 19, and 24 form the function-held-out validation split.  Functions,
rather than randomly selected transformed instances, define the grouping
variable.  This partition addresses evidence that instance-level splits can
overstate algorithm-selection generalization
\cite{cenikjLandscapeFeaturesSingleobjective2025}.  Both sets use dimensions
$d\in\{10,20,40\}$, instances $1,2,3$, optimizer seeds $1,\ldots,30$, population
size 40, and total budget $B=1000d$.  The portfolio contains DE, PSO, CMA-ES,
and SHADE, giving 54 training and 18 validation function--dimension
combinations.

The single best solver (SBS) is estimated exclusively from terminal outcomes at
$FE=B$.  Each raw gap is clipped with the suite endpoints and transformed to
$\log_{10}$ gap.  For each algorithm, outcomes are averaged with equal weight
from run to problem (dimension--instance pair) to function; the algorithm with
the smallest function-balanced mean is selected.  A tie is resolved in the
prespecified order DE, PSO, CMA-ES, SHADE.  The procedure is repeated using only
the fit functions of every outer and inner fold.  It does not use average ranks,
and intermediate decision states do not contribute.

BBOB validation is a previously inspected internal development-evaluation set.
Historical model comparison, tuning, and ablation work examined its outcomes,
so excluding it from the current query selection, preprocessing, model-family
selection, input-group comparison, threshold fitting, and score-neighborhood
definition does not restore independent-confirmation status.  Its six
functions are six top-level evaluation units.  Its role is estimation-first:
all six function-level effects, the fixed-set mean, and conditional intervals
are reported, but its results do not trigger model reselection or binary claims
based on a $p$-value threshold.

\subsection{External benchmarks}

External evaluation is organized as non-pooled, suite-specific analyses.  The
current CEC2017 configuration specifies dimensions 10, 30, and 50, seeds
$1,\ldots,30$, population size 40, and budget $B=1000d$.  Its function list is
not yet an executable manuscript commitment: the current F1--F29 list includes
F2 and excludes F30, while the project contains no source establishing that
choice for the implementation in use.  The F2/F30 treatment must be reconciled
with the implementation and benchmark definition before any further evaluation
under the current protocol.  The four-algorithm portfolio and dynamic opportunity protocol remain
unchanged, and all learned components are then held fixed at their
BBOB-training estimates.

CEC2022 and engineering problems form separate components of RQ4.  Their
problem sets, dimensions, bounds, budgets, target errors, error floors and
caps, wall-time limits, and official constraint-handling rules are specified
before any policy outcomes are inspected.  A run that raises an optimizer
exception, returns a non-finite objective value, exceeds its fixed wall-time
limit, or violates a suite-defined completion condition is retained as a run
failure.  A target hit observed before that failure remains a target hit for
standard ERT, while path completion is false and completion-conditioned
endpoint success is false.  A failed path with no observed hit contributes its
full planned budget to ERT.  The path receives the prespecified suite-level
worst rank and capped error for summaries that require a finite value; the cap
is never estimated from observed policy contrasts.

For every external suite, an attempted query whose descriptor group cannot be
computed remains in the coverage denominator and is recorded as a query
failure.  The evaluations and time already spent on that query remain charged,
no replacement descriptor group is invented, and the optimizer continues the
prefix action with its remaining budget.  Individual undefined descriptor
values may be imputed only with frozen BBOB-training medians; a group-level
extraction failure is not converted into a successful query by imputation.
Missing benchmark implementations or unexecuted problems are reported as
missing coverage and cannot be counted as completed evaluation.  Previously
inspected BBOB validation, CEC2017, prospective CEC2022, and engineering
estimates are reported separately;
neither partial coverage nor agreement on a subset of suites is interpreted as
unconditional cross-benchmark transfer.

For BBOB training/validation and CEC2017, the prespecified values are a raw-gap
floor of $10^{-12}$, a raw-gap and failure-loss cap of $10^{20}$, success target
$10^{-8}$, and a 3600-second state--action timeout.  First hits are checked after
every objective evaluation, including evaluations in a query sample.  A timeout
is retained as path failure.  It does not erase an earlier observed hit;
standard ERT uses that hit, whereas an unhit path contributes its full planned
budget.  Path completion and completion-conditioned endpoint success are
reported separately.  CEC2022 and engineering problems cannot be run until
analogous endpoints and their constraint-handling rules have been fixed.

For every suite and endpoint, the attempted denominator and coverage are shown
beside the complete-pair estimate.  Unclosed pairs receive two prespecified
extreme assignments: gap uses the suite floor and cap; target hit uses one and
zero; an unsuccessful ERT contribution uses the full budget; adverse runtime
uses the timeout; and favorable runtime uses the smallest strictly positive
runtime among that suite's complete pairs, with this rule frozen before a run.
Utility is recomputed from the same extreme gap and runtime endpoints rather
than assigned an arbitrary value.  A scientific path failure already retained
through a cap or timeout is an observed outcome, not a missing pair.  If either
extreme analysis changes the effect direction or the interval's position
relative to the operational tolerance, or if coverage is below 95\%, the
corresponding conclusion is not established.

The cheap and standard queries use the same evaluated $(X,y)$ sample.  The
cheap list excludes the response median and IQR because the common median/IQR
preprocessing makes them constants zero and one.  Their removal changes neither
the sample nor the action outcomes.  Sharing the sample yields
a paired comparison of descriptor representations at a common FE cost.  The
broad query uses an independent $100d$ design.  The standard configuration
comprises PCA, nearest-better clustering, dispersion, information-content, and
ELA-distribution groups.  The broad configuration adds ELA level-set and
sample-derived fitness--distance-correlation groups, while excluding the full
quadratic meta-model group, cell mapping, and feature groups that require
additional objective evaluations.  Query sample points are not inserted into
the optimizer population.  Each query defines a separate downstream selector,
utility target, decision model, threshold, baseline comparison, and external
evaluation; observations from different query identities or FE designs are not
pooled.

Each static problem and sample design has one problem-keyed LHS realization,
shared across optimizer seeds, decision states, and actions.  The primary
estimands are therefore conditional on these fixed query realizations; without
multiple independent LHS replicates they do not average over query-sampling
randomness.  Additional replicates could only enter as a prespecified
sampling-robustness sensitivity, with replicate identifiers included in pairing
and hierarchical analysis.  Standard versus broad changes representation,
sample size, sample realization, and action budget simultaneously, so that
contrast is interpreted only at the whole-query-configuration level.

\subsection{Dynamic decision opportunities}

Offline label construction and policy evaluation use the same dynamic
budget--event sampling scheme.  Complete native updates are monitored when they
cross a grid from $0.20B$ to $0.60B$ in increments of $0.01B$.  The mandatory
budget milestones are 0.20, 0.22, 0.24, 0.26, 0.28, 0.30, 0.34, 0.38, 0.42,
0.46, 0.50, and 0.60.  Five event types may add decision states: resumed strict
improvement; a stagnation span reaching $0.05B$; an absolute medium-window
covariance-effective-rank change reaching $0.20$; unit-cube elite-centroid
displacement divided by $\sqrt{d}$ reaching $0.05$; and relative diversity
recovery reaching $0.10$.  Covariance-effective-rank-change, elite-migration,
and diversity-recovery events rearm when their respective quantities fall below
$0.10$, $0.025$, and $0.05$.  Stagnation rearms after a new strict best-so-far
improvement, whereas resumed improvement rearms when short-window improvement
frequency returns to zero.

Each complete update that crosses one or more monitor points is evaluated once.
If a crossing includes a milestone, the event is associated with that milestone
and does not consume an event-only quota or spacing anchor.  Otherwise, the most
recent crossed monitor point supplies the nominal location.  Each of the early,
middle, and late phases admits at most two event-only states, with at least
$0.02B$ separation in realized FE ratio.  Suppressed crossings still update
event rearming.  This scheme admits 12--18 states per run.  All accepted states
are retained for the full B3 controller, but they are not treated as independent
or globally equal-weight observations.  The realized $FE/B$ represents elapsed
budget and is the sole T0 covariate; exact integer FE aligns shared-state
outcomes.  Nominal milestones and native-update counts describe the sampling
process but do not add Decision Model inputs.  T0 is fitted, thresholded, and
evaluated only on the twelve mandatory milestone states.  Whenever B3 is
compared directly with T0 as an information ablation, the B3 estimate is
recomputed on the same milestone rows; event-only opportunities therefore
cannot disclose behavioral event occurrence to the time-only comparator.

\subsection{Matched states and analysis population}

The analysis pairs all alternatives at an identical complete native state.
For each eligible state, it combines permutation-invariant behavior, the shared
$50d$ and independent $100d$ LHS samples, the three descriptor representations,
and the query-budget-specific outcome of every eligible action.  A trajectory
enters an estimand only when both its complete native history and terminal
outcome satisfy the same state definition.  Query-specific selection
references, utilities, decision models, and policies are estimated from these
matched observations under the function-group partitions described above.

The primary Decision Model population contains states for which both the prefix
and default algorithm equal the training SBS and the no-query path continues
that optimizer.  States generated from the other prefix algorithms are reserved
for cross-prefix robustness, leave-one-prefix-algorithm-out analysis, and tests
of portability across the four search processes.  They are excluded from
primary model fitting, threshold fitting, and primary result aggregation.

Offline generation is explicitly two-stage.  The four-action matrices and
FE=0 four-algorithm outcomes are each generated once to fit fold-specific
selectors.  These prespecified Stage-A executions also fix terminal gap,
observed first hit, target-hit status, path completion,
completion-conditioned endpoint success, planned FE, effective FE, and
scientific failure status.
Those selectors emit a replay plan; only then are their selected
Skip, Query, and Behavior-only paths from the corresponding decision state to
termination executed three prespecified times with cyclic path order.  The
pre-run AAS and complete online-policy paths use the same division: one Stage-A
scientific execution and three Stage-B timing replays from FE zero.  Stage B
provides only wall-clock measurements and repetition-level status, effective
FE, timeout, completion, and failure diagnostics; it cannot overwrite a
Stage-A scientific endpoint.  A completed replay contributes its raw observed
time to the censored time, whereas a timed-out or failed replay contributes the
larger of its raw time and role-specific timeout.  Utility uses the median of
the three censored times; the raw observed median is diagnostic only.
Path-identity consistency, consistency among completed replays, and consistency
between Stage A and completed replays are stored separately, as are within-Stage-B
status instability and Stage-A/Stage-B completion instability.  No repetition
is selectively rerun after its time or status is observed.  Utility
is constructed after these replays from Stage-A performance and Stage-B
state-conditional future-path times for Skip, Query, and Behavior-only.  The
replay plan is a scientific dependency, not an optional timing pass: without an
offline decision-state-to-terminal producer and a fold-role-complete plan,
formal data generation does not begin.

\subsection{Comparison policies}

Nine prespecified comparison roles are retained because they answer different
benchmarking questions.  Every sequential decision policy is a first-trigger
policy: it scans its eligible opportunities in increasing exact FE order, can
trigger at most once in a run, and contributes only the Utility and terminal
outcome associated with that first trigger.  Once a query or behavior-only
handoff has been initiated, later states in that run are not additional calls,
observations, or policy rewards.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Never Query}: continue the complete SBS state natively and incur no query acquisition cost.
  \item \textbf{Always Query}: execute the fixed query at the first accepted decision opportunity and invoke the same downstream selector used by the proposed policy.
  \item \textbf{Matched-rate Random Analysis}: freeze the proposed controller's run-level call rate and empirical first-trigger FE-ratio distribution from the complete BBOB-training fold-specific OOF chain.  At the start of a run, one Bernoulli draw determines whether a call is planned; if so, a target ratio is drawn and the first eligible opportunity not earlier than that target is used.  The run does not call when no such opportunity exists.  Thirty explicit \texttt{SeedSequence} streams are averaged within the same scientific run before problem/function aggregation; they are not independent scientific replications.  Neither a constant opportunity hazard nor a state drawn uniformly after observing the complete opportunity set is used.
  \item \textbf{FE-zero query-only, sample-isolated pre-run AAS}: before any portfolio optimizer update, acquire the fixed LHS query, compute its descriptors, and use a separately trained query-only selector to choose one portfolio algorithm for the remaining budget.  The query consumes its configured FE and runtime, its sample neither initializes nor augments the optimizer population, and the selected algorithm starts from its fresh native initialization with budget $B-FE_q$.  The selector uses query descriptors and static remaining-budget information but no trajectory behavior.  For explicit relation accounting, the prefix and selected algorithms are set equal, selected-equals-prefix is true, handoff-required is false, handoff type is fresh-optimizer-initialization, and the default/no-query algorithm is the corresponding fold SBS.  This baseline is not identified with the statewise Always Query policy, and its interpretation is conditional on the repository's fixed implementations, parameters, and population size 40.
  \item \textbf{SBS}: use the training-derived static default defined above.  In the primary population, the prefix and default are this same SBS, so its native complete-budget path coincides with Never Query; the separate name preserves its role as the standard algorithm-selection reference rather than introducing another online trajectory.
  \item \textbf{VBS}: for each static problem defined by function, instance, and dimension, average each algorithm's complete-budget clipped $\log_{10}$ gap over optimizer seeds, select the algorithm with the smallest mean (ties follow DE, PSO, CMA-ES, SHADE), and then retain that algorithm's paired seed-level outcomes for aggregation.  It never chooses an algorithm separately for each seed.  It is a non-deployable problem-level hindsight reference, does not necessarily upper-bound a dynamic policy run by run, and is distinct from the statewise best observed action.
  \item \textbf{Milestone-only Time Controller}: use the selected Decision Model family and the same training-only threshold procedure as the full controller, with input restricted to $\{FE/B\}$.  It is fitted and checked only at the twelve mandatory milestones and stops after its first trigger.
  \item \textbf{Self-thresholded Behavior-only Selection}: fit the same frozen model family to $U_b$ and obtain its own train-only first-trigger threshold.  At its first crossing, the full-budget Behavior-only selector chooses the continuation or handoff action; query-call rate and query FEs are zero, while trigger and handoff rates remain reportable.
  \item \textbf{Decision-before-Feature}: use B3 Behavior with the selected model and the threshold estimated from full-training fold-specific upstream OOF predictions; acquire the query only at the first threshold crossing and invoke the full state-plus-query selector once.
\end{itemize}
In the primary population, Never Query and SBS yield the same native terminal
outcome and are therefore reported once as \emph{Never Query / SBS}.  The other
seven roles produce distinct outcome rows, giving eight distinct rows for the
nine roles.  Traditional pre-run AAS remains separate from Always Query because
it queries before an optimizer trajectory exists and commits to one algorithm.
For every applicable endpoint, the shared Never Query / SBS outcome enters
summaries and multiplicity counts once.

All methods receive the same total FE budget, portfolio, benchmark bounds, and
suite-specific stopping rules.  Sequential full-state policies use the same
accepted opportunity stream, except that the time-only controller is
deliberately restricted to milestones.  Query policies use the same query
definition and full downstream selector, and statewise handoffs use the same
population-transfer rule.  The pre-run AAS and behavior-only selectors are
  separate prespecified estimators because their available information and action
  budgets differ.  A distinct matched-trigger Behavior-only diagnostic acts at
  the proposed controller's trigger solely to compute $I_q$; it is not the
  self-thresholded baseline.  Query-adjusted state-only, query-only, Behavior-only,
  and full selector regressions remain component diagnostics in addition to the
  policy-level baseline.

\subsection{Evaluation measures}

Decision-model family selection uses nested grouped-by-function OOF mean decision
utility.  AUROC, average precision, and Spearman correlation are secondary
score-level measures.  Continuous-utility RMSE is reported only for Ridge;
classification probabilities and discriminant scores are not compared with
continuous utility using RMSE.

Training outer OOF outcomes select among the three prespecified candidates and
support development diagnostics only.  Once the largest-OOF candidate is
selected, the same OOF outcomes are not an unbiased estimate of the selected
procedure.  The prespecified RQ2 contrast---milestone-only B3 minus
milestone-only T0---is reported on BBOB validation only as a post-development
internal finite-set estimate, with each external suite reported separately.
Candidate pairwise contrasts do not enter an RQ2 Holm family.

The primary fitting estimand gives equal weight successively to functions,
fixed dimension strata, static problems, and optimizer runs, then divides each
run's weight equally among its eligible states.  Fold-local row weights are
normalized to mean one and cannot depend on holdout rows or outcome direction.
Unit weight per state row is retained only as a sensitivity analysis.  The
Decision and Selector estimators have not yet been wired to these cluster-balanced
weights, which is a formal pre-run blocker; nested first-trigger evaluation does
not by itself correct fitting weights.

Policy-level reporting includes run-call rate, first-trigger precision, mean
joint decision Utility, matched-trigger $I_q$, and terminal outcomes.  If
$J_r^\pi$ is policy $\pi$'s first trigger, its run Utility is
\begin{equation}
  U_r^\pi=
  \begin{cases}
    U_q^{\mathrm{joint}}(S_{r,J_r^\pi}), & J_r^\pi\text{ exists},\\
    0, & \text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:policy-decision-utility}
\end{equation}
The reported mean aggregates $U_r^\pi$ equally from run to problem to function.
Never Query has zero query-decision Utility by definition; the SBS role and VBS
remain not applicable for this quantity.  Mean observed Utility conditional on
calling is reported separately.

All sequential policies share one hindsight capture reference from the native
SBS/default trajectory,
\begin{equation}
  H_r=\max_t\max\{U_q^{\mathrm{joint}}(S_{r,t}),0\}.
\end{equation}
Policy capture uses only its first trigger,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{utility\ capture}(\pi)=
  \frac{\sum_r\max\{U_q^{\mathrm{joint}}(S_{r,J_r^\pi}),0\}}
       {\sum_r H_r},
  \label{eq:utility-capture}
\end{equation}
with zero numerator for an untriggered run.  Weighted numerator and denominator,
their ratio, and the number of $H_r=0$ runs are reported.  Calls with
$U_q^{\mathrm{joint}}\le0$ are summarized by count, within-call proportion, and
accumulated Utility loss.  A state-score ranking diagnostic, if shown, is not
called policy capture.

Policy-level endpoints comprise terminal clipped $\log_{10}$ gap,
observed target-hit rate, completion-conditioned endpoint-success rate,
expected running time (ERT), total and query FEs,
FE-zero-to-terminal wall-clock time, behavior-extraction time,
controller-inference time, query
sampling and descriptor-computation time, downstream-selection latency, and
peak memory.  These full-run wall-clock values include the realized prefix and
are reported separately from the decision-state-to-terminal future-path times
that enter $U_q^{\mathrm{joint}}$, $U_b$, and $I_q$.  Success targets are suite specific and are fixed independently
of the observed policy contrasts.  Cost--performance reporting retains all
nine prespecified comparison roles but uses eight distinct primary-population
outcomes because Never Query and SBS share one native outcome.  Traditional
pre-run AAS remains distinct from Always Query.

\subsection{Statistical analysis}

Repeated states from one trajectory are not independent replicates.  The
aggregation hierarchy is state, run, static problem, fixed dimension stratum,
and function.  RQ1 first averages the complete eligible-state sequence within
each run; policy endpoints start from one first-trigger outcome per run.
Paired optimizer seeds are averaged within each static
function--dimension--instance problem, static problems within each
function--dimension stratum, and the fixed 10D, 20D, and 40D strata within each
function.  The benchmark function is the highest aggregation stratum.

The BBOB-validation estimand is the equally weighted finite-set mean for
the six prespecified functions F5, F9, F13, F14, F19, and F24, conditional on
those functions and the fixed dimensions and instances.  It is a previously
inspected post-development internal estimate; it neither treats the six
functions or three transformed instances as probability samples nor supports
inference to a function or transformed-instance superpopulation.  The current
choice of three instances and 30 optimizer seeds has no repository-local
precision or power justification; it is a fixed development design, not
evidence that a target half-width has been attained.  Repetition counts for
prospective CEC2022 and engineering evaluations must receive an explicit
precision target and be fixed before their outcomes are observed.

Primary intervals use 10,000 conditional paired hierarchical-bootstrap
resamples.  Every resample retains all six validation functions, every fixed
dimension, and every static problem for instances 1--3; paired optimizer seeds
are resampled only within each fixed static problem.  An RQ1 seed/run retains
its complete ordered state sequence as one cluster.  Function
resampling is reported only as a separately labelled function-composition
sensitivity and never replaces the primary 95\% interval.

ERT uses a dedicated paired ratio bootstrap rather than treating a run-level
ERT contribution as an ordinary mean.  Within each bootstrap replicate and
each function--dimension cell, static problems remain fixed and paired
optimizer runs are resampled within each problem.  The FE numerator and
target-hit count are recomputed for each policy,
and the treatment-to-reference $\log_{10}$ ERT ratio is formed before fixed
dimensions and functions are equally averaged.  A one-sided zero-hit cell is
retained as signed infinity and a two-sided zero-hit cell is undefined; neither
is deleted, and either makes the corresponding interval unestablished.  The
suite-level consumer must combine this ratio analysis with attempted coverage
and the two failure-sensitivity assignments before any suite conclusion is
reported.

The numerical margins---mean Utility difference $[-0.01,0.01]$, mean
$\log_{10}$-gap difference $[-0.05,0.05]$, geometric-mean runtime ratio
$[0.95,1.05]$, and absolute call/target-hit-rate difference $[-0.05,0.05]$---are
study-specific operational tolerances, not domain-grounded practical-importance
bounds.  Primary conditional intervals use 95\% coverage.  Bonferroni
hierarchical-bootstrap intervals are descriptive sensitivity summaries of
where a prespecified family lies relative to these tolerances.  For $m$
contrasts, each uses two-sided level $1-0.05/m$, giving family-wise 95\%
coverage.  Neither interval produces a
confirmatory equivalence claim.  Offset between gap and runtime inside Utility
does not establish either endpoint, and lack of significance does not establish
equivalence.

Prespecified paired contrasts use function-level sign-flip summaries under the
null assumption that the six fixed function-effect signs are exchangeable.
BBOB validation enumerates all $2^6=64$ sign vectors, so the smallest two-sided
exact raw $p$ value is $2/64=0.03125$.  This summarizes sign consistency within
the fixed set and does not create function-superpopulation inference.

Auxiliary Holm families are fixed separately by RQ and endpoint.  RQ2 has one
prespecified internal contrast, BBOB-validation milestone-only B3 versus T0; the three
pairwise B3 candidate comparisons are selection diagnostics, not RQ2 family
members.  RQ3 compares the proposed policy with
Never/SBS, Always Query, matched-rate Random, pre-run AAS, milestone-only T0,
and self-thresholded Behavior-only; RQ5 contains the six prespecified feature
increments.  RQ4 does not treat the four suites as a four-contrast family:
each suite and endpoint has a separate finite-set estimate, and any within-suite
multiplicity scope must be fixed before that suite's first outcome.  Suites and
endpoints are not pooled.  VBS, query-configuration and
$\lambda_T$ robustness, matched-trigger $I_q$, and selector diagnostics are not
members of these prespecified auxiliary Holm families.  Analytically identical
Never Query/SBS outcomes
enter each summary and multiplicity count once.

On BBOB validation, the RQ3 and RQ5 Holm families each contain six contrasts.
Their smallest possible adjusted $p$ value is therefore
$6(2/64)=0.1875$.  Neither can reject at 0.05, regardless of the observed
effect pattern.  We report raw and
adjusted $p$ values only for transparency; function-specific effects, the fixed
six-function mean, and its conditional interval are the primary evidence, and
non-rejection is not interpreted as equivalence or absence of an effect.
