\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The present study separates two decisions that are usually collapsed in
feature-based algorithm selection: whether to execute an evaluated fixed
landscape query and, conditional on execution, which portfolio action to use.
This formulation is methodological and does not by itself establish that the
query should be skipped or executed in any empirical regime.

\subsection{When is the evaluated query worth executing?}

\TBD{TBD-DISC-01} The statewise utility distribution has not yet been
estimated.  Its eventual interpretation is conditional on the evaluated query,
utility definition, effect direction, interval, and state coverage.  The joint
Query utility, full-budget Behavior-only utility, and their operational
increment answer different questions and must be reported separately.  The
continuation-only predictive increment further excludes the sample best and
acquisition cost; it cannot replace the operational comparison.  None of these
quantities can be extended from the fixed low-cost query to landscape analysis
in general.

\subsection{Information in algorithm-agnostic search behavior}

\TBD{TBD-DISC-02} The RQ2 comparison between T0 and the behavior groups remains
undetermined pending nested BBOB-train grouped-by-function OOF estimates and
suite-specific evaluation.  Decision utility, AUROC, Average Precision, Spearman correlation,
and Ridge RMSE support different aspects of the comparison; disagreement among
them precludes a single undifferentiated predictive claim.

\subsection{End-to-end resource and performance trade-offs}

\TBD{TBD-DISC-03} The practical resource--performance trade-off is
undetermined.  Its interpretation requires joint evidence from equal-total-FE
final performance, decision-state-to-terminal future-path time, complete
FE-zero-to-terminal policy wall-clock, measured query and inference time,
query-call rate, and the cost of calls with non-positive observed utility.
The conditional future-path time treats the shared prefix as a sunk cost; the
full-run endpoint includes that prefix.  They are not interchangeable.  Fewer
calls alone would not establish better resource-aware optimization.

\subsection{Development sets and cross-benchmark scope}

\TBD{TBD-DISC-04} Effects under the current protocol on previously inspected
BBOB validation and CEC2017, and on prospective CEC2022 and engineering
problems, remain unestimated and provide no current evidence of generalization.
Any eventual interpretation is suite-specific; query-extraction failures,
divergent effect directions, or incomplete coverage narrow its scope.
The BBOB-validation estimand is specifically the equally weighted mean of its
six fixed functions.  It is a post-development internal finite-set estimate,
not an independent confirmation or a sample from a function superpopulation.
Function-resampling results, if shown, are composition sensitivities only.

\subsection{Search maturity and behavior interpretation}

\TBD{TBD-DISC-05} The RQ5 ablation, coefficient or discriminant-direction
stability, and maturity--utility association remain unestimated.  Coefficients, discriminant
directions, and correlations have a predictive rather than causal
interpretation; a non-monotonic relationship would require support from its
prespecified fit, interval, and stability analysis.

\subsection{Limitations and validity boundaries}

The primary estimand is query-specific: it concerns
\texttt{descriptor\_cheap\_invariant}, a 14-dimensional custom low-cost descriptor query.
Two response summaries that become constants under the common median/IQR
preprocessing are intentionally excluded; this changes neither the evaluated
sample nor its FE cost.
The prespecified standard and broad \texttt{pflacco} queries can assess
configuration robustness but cannot justify claims
about untested landscape representations.  Each problem/sample design uses one
fixed problem-keyed LHS realization, so inference is conditional on those
realizations rather than averaged over sampling randomness.  Moreover,
standard versus broad changes representation, sample size, sample realization,
and budget together and cannot identify their separate contributions.  The
absence of a matched-acquisition state-only operational path likewise prevents
descriptor-only attribution of $I_q$.  The Selection Reference is a fixed
downstream component rather than a claimed algorithm-selection contribution,
and its selector regret must remain visible when interpreting query utility.
The static VBS is selected at the problem level after averaging each algorithm
over optimizer seeds; it never selects a different algorithm for every seed and
is distinct from the statewise best observed action.
The main Decision Model is trained offline with a BBOB grouped-by-function
split; the historical grouping key does not denote a classic landscape family.  The
study does not evaluate online controller training as a primary design.  Its
primary protocol uses function--dimension--static-problem--run-balanced weights
and divides run weight across eligible states.  The estimator wiring is not yet
implemented; nested first-trigger evaluation does not remove this fitting risk.
Unweighted state-row fitting is retained only as a sensitivity analysis.
All AAS and portfolio conclusions are conditional on the repository's frozen
DE, PSO, CMA-ES, and SHADE implementations, parameters, and population size 40.

\TBD{TBD-DISC-06} The empirical limitations remain to be quantified through
trajectory and action-loss coverage, query-extraction failures, function-level
dependence, population-transfer frequency, and sensitivity to the three
prespecified query configurations and utility-cost settings.
